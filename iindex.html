About the Author

Lev (Leo) Tolstoy, (1828-1910)—a Russian writer, philosopher, and social thinker, was widely regarded as one of the greatest novelists of all time. His masterpieces, War and Peace (1869) and Anna Karenina (1877), are celebrated for their psychological depth, historical accuracy, and profound philosophical insights.

Tolstoy's early works, such as Childhood, Boyhood, and Youth (1852-1856) and Sevastopol Sketches (1855), reflect his personal experiences and observations of war. However, he later underwent a spiritual transformation, leading to his advocacy of pacifism, Christian anarchism, and simple living, as seen in The Kingdom of God Is Within You (1894). His later fiction, including The Death of Ivan Ilyich (1886) and Resurrection (1899), critiques social injustice and explores existential themes.

Tolstoy's ideas on nonviolence influenced leaders like Mahatma Gandhi and Martin Luther King Jr. Despite conflicts with the Russian Orthodox Church, his moral and literary legacy remains unparalleled in world literature.

About the Text

"Three Questions" is a short story by Leo Tolstoy, first published in 1885 as part of his collection What Men Live By, and Other Tales. Through the tale of a Tsar who wanted to know the nature of appropriate counsel and action, Tolstoy reveals an illuminating notion about the duties of Man. This simple tale is loaded with deep thoughts on the way human beings should conduct their actions. The story reflects Tolstoy's philosophical and moral beliefs, particularly his ideas on wisdom, time, and human responsibility.
